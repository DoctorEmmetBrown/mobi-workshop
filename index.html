Modulation-Based Imaging Workshop

Phase imaging across all wavelengths

One week in the French Alps for the X-ray, optics and visible-light communities that work with modulation: gratings, masks, speckle, structured light. Talks in the morning. The rest of the day, a table big enough for everyone.

Why this workshop

Most meetings reward finished work.

Our field already has good conferences, and they are built around polished, best results. They leave little room for the other half of research: the dataset that behaves oddly, the method that works on one set-up and not on the next, the question two groups have been asking without knowing it.

MoBI 2027 is built around that half. It takes its spirit from scientific retreats and from the LINX initiative (Inserm, Monash, University of Canterbury), which already works this way. The location is remote on purpose. People get time to think, and the group is small enough that you can talk to everyone.

Scope

The same questions, at every wavelength.

Modulation-based imaging is not specific to X-rays. A grating in a synchrotron beam, a diffuser in front of a camera, a structured illumination in a microscope: each shapes a wave and reads the result back. Coherence, modulator design and signal retrieval come up in every community, usually in different words.

Monday's two tutorials, one per community, give everyone the vocabulary of the other side before the week starts in earnest.

  1. X-raysynchrotron, microfocus, lab sources
  2. EUVsoft X-ray, XUV
  3. Visiblelasers, LEDs, microscopy, wavefront sensing
  4. Beyondterahertz, neutrons, other probes

Afternoons

Afternoons are for unfinished work.

Everyone has something that does not quite work yet: a measurement that drifts, a model that holds until it doesn't, a result nobody in the lab could explain. Here, that is good material.

On Tuesday each participant gets five minutes to put one of these on a shared board. Groups form around the questions that draw people in, and they are mixed on purpose. The person who has already met your difficulty is often working at another wavelength, under another name.

  1. Tue · 14:30

    Pitch

    About twelve lightning talks of five minutes. Then an open discussion, with the board filling up.

  2. Tue · 16:30

    Gather

    Groups of X-ray and visible-light people. Each defines a question, the data or simulation it needs, and what it hopes to show on Friday.

  3. Wed · 14:00

    Dig in

    Round two, with the morning's modulator discussions fresh. A short break is left to each group, then the hike.

  4. Thu · 14:30

    Try

    Shared datasets, real and simulated, run through several methods with a common set of figures of merit.

  5. Fri · 14:30

    Share

    Ten minutes per group, then a roadmap: shared resources, benchmarks and next steps.

Questions already on the board

A first list from the scientific committee, to seed the week. It grows with the open problems you send through the call for abstracts. Yours may not be on it yet.

Shared tools · Both communities

A repository of test samples and reference datasets?

Comparing methods needs common inputs with a known answer. A sample described by its projected thickness and the structure of its scatterers can be simulated at any wavelength; real acquisitions of well-characterised phantoms would complete it.

Shared tools · Both communities

Can we develop a common library of algorithms?

Retrieval methods are implemented many times, in different languages and with different conventions. A shared open-source library with a common data model would help both communities.

Shared tools · Both communities

Which figures of merit and which protocol should we use to compare methods?

Sensitivity, resolution, noise, bias and computation time are reported differently from one paper to the next. A handful of metrics, measured on the same data, would turn shared data and code into a benchmark.

Light & coherence · Both communities

How should we measure and report the coherence a MoBI system actually sees?

Spatial and temporal coherence are described with different conventions in X-ray and in optics, and are rarely reported next to the results.

Light & coherence · Both communities

How much coherence is enough?

Many forward models assume full coherence, while real beams are only partially coherent. Where does the coherent model stop being valid, and what does a partially coherent model cost?

Light & coherence · Both communities

Can we do quantitative MoBI with broadband illumination?

Polychromatic X-ray beams and white-light sources both blur the modulation and can bias the retrieved signals. Which corrections transfer from one field to the other?

Light & coherence

How do we “transfer” the interferometry theory across wavelengths?

Under visible illumination, objects are often pure phase objects; under X-ray illumination there is always some attenuation. Approximations valid in the visible cannot always be made with X-rays.

The modulator · Both communities

What is the best modulation topology?

Gratings, checkerboards, random diffusers and speckle, coded or structured patterns: each is defended on its own terms. A fair comparison needs common criteria.

The modulator · Both communities

How do we maximise the information gained per photon?

Absorbing modulators throw photons away; phase modulators and scatterers keep them but are harder to model. Dose for X-ray, light budget for the visible: the same trade-off.

The modulator · Both communities

How do we characterise a modulator and describe it to the model?

Real modulators deviate from their design in profile, period and stability. How accurately must we know them, and how do we measure them in practice?

The modulator · Both communities

Can we agree on a trusted end-to-end simulator?

Simulation is used in both communities, but each validates it against different experiments. A common validation case would show which simulators can be trusted for design.

Retrieval & quantification · Both communities

Phase integration: how do we stay quantitative?

Integrating phase gradients is sensitive to noise, boundary conditions and low spatial frequencies. Reconstructing a wavefront from slopes is also a classical problem in optics.

Retrieval & quantification · Both communities

How do we take noise into account efficiently?

Noise enters the forward model, the retrieval and the regularisation. How do we get useful uncertainty estimates without heavy Monte Carlo runs?

Retrieval & quantification · X-ray first

What exactly does dark-field measure, and can it be compared between set-ups?

The signal depends on the set-up (propagation distance, modulation scale, wavelength) as well as on the sample. Which sample-intrinsic quantity can we extract?

Retrieval & quantification · Both communities

How do we calibrate, and can we calibrate less?

Modulator, detector response, geometry, wavelength: which of these can be recovered from the data itself, and which need a dedicated measurement?

Pushing the limits · Both communities

What can spectral information add?

Energy-resolved detectors and hyperspectral cameras can separate absorption, phase, scattering and materials. What is gained over a single band, and at what cost?

Pushing the limits · Both communities

What are the real limits of super-resolution with modulation?

Modulation can encode information below the pixel size. What sets the limit, and when is a claimed gain in resolution real?

Pushing the limits · X-ray first

How do we represent and interpret directional, multi-contrast signals?

Dark-field and scattering can depend on direction and scale. How do we describe them compactly, and how do band-resolved descriptions compare with tensor-like ones?

Pushing the limits · Visible first

Beyond phase and beyond X-rays: which other contrasts and wavelengths are within reach?

Polarisation-sensitive contrast in the visible may have equivalents elsewhere, and EUV, terahertz or neutrons could use the same ideas. What transfers, and what must change?

Pushing the limits · Both communities

Can we trust learning-based retrieval for quantitative work?

Networks trained on simulations or limited data can look convincing and still be biased. How do we validate them, and what should be reported with the results?

Pushing the limits · X-ray first

How can we increase the acquisition speed?

A balance between noise, the information we can still extract, and grating optimisation.

Bring the one that keeps you up. Someone here has probably seen it from the other side.

What can come out of a week like this

  • Reference phantoms and datasets that anyone can use to test a method on common ground.
  • A short benchmark protocol, with agreed figures of merit, that groups can run on their own set-up or software.
  • New collaborators from the other side of the field, and a roadmap for what to do next together. These depend on who comes and what they bring.

Place

Aussois, at the edge of the Vanoise.

The Centre Paul-Langevin (CAES du CNRS) is a recently refurbished centre at about 1,500 m, in a south-facing Savoie village near the Italian border. Rooms, restaurant, bar and meeting spaces are all on site, with a 200-seat auditorium and many breakout rooms, so the day does not scatter when a session ends.

In early June the valley is green, and the trails of the Vanoise National Park start at the door.
